How to Clean and Maintain a Blender: The Complete Care Guide

A practical after-use routine, deep-cleaning schedule and part-replacement plan that keeps a blender performing like new for years.

Updated 2026-08-18 · Research-led editorial guide · As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases.

Quick answer: Wash the parts food touches after every use, keep the powered body dry and wipe-only, and put the deeper jobs on a monthly calendar. Most blenders "failures" are maintenance debts coming due.

A blender that smells like old smoothie, a jar gone permanently cloudy, or a blade that wobbles are all preventable outcomes. Blender care is genuinely quick—the machine can wash itself in under a minute—but the details around the blade assembly, the gasket, and what you ask the motor to do decide whether it lasts three years or fifteen.

The after-every-use routine

1. Run the self-clean cycle immediately after use

Fill the jar halfway with warm water, add one drop of dish soap, and blend on high for 30 to 60 seconds. Rinse. Doing this before residue dries is 90 percent of blender cleaning.

2. Rinse the lid and center cap separately

Smoothie residue hides under the lid gasket and inside the removable center cap. Pull them apart and rinse; this is where mold starts.

3. Wipe the base and control panel

Drips run down the jar onto the base every session. A damp cloth keeps the buttons and the jar pad clean; never immerse the motor base.

4. Clean the jar pad and coupling area

Sticky residue where the jar seats can make the jar sit crooked and the drive coupling wear unevenly. Wipe it dry after washing.

5. Air-dry the jar upside down

Trapped water at the blade assembly breeds odor and can corrode the bearing area. Drain fully before storing with the lid off.

Deep-cleaning jobs worth calendaring

Deep-clean cloudy or smelly jars

Blend equal parts water and white vinegar, or a jar of warm water with a tablespoon of baking soda, let it sit 30 minutes, then wash normally. Cloudiness from mineral film lifts; permanent etching from dishwashers does not.

Service the blade gasket

On blenders with removable blade assemblies, unscrew the collar occasionally and wash the rubber gasket by hand. A dried, cracked, or swollen gasket is the cause of most jar leaks—and it costs a few dollars to replace.

Check for bearing wear

Spin the blade by hand when clean and dry. Grinding, wobble, or black dust at the jar bottom means the bearing is failing; replace the blade assembly before it fails mid-blend.

What quietly ruins blenders

Leaving the blend to dry

Dried smoothie in blade crevices takes ten times the effort of a rinse now, and repeated soaking to remove it is what gets water into the bearing.

Dishwashing jars not rated for it

Heated dry cycles cloud and craze plastic jars permanently. Even for rated jars, hand washing keeps measurement markings and clarity intact far longer.

Blending hot liquids with a sealed lid

Steam pressure blows lids off and sprays scalding liquid. Fill half full, remove the center cap, cover with a folded towel, and start slow—or use a blender with a vented lid designed for hot soup.

Reaching in around the blades

Use a brush or the self-clean cycle. Blender blades cause a remarkable number of kitchen injuries during cleaning, not blending.

Wear parts and when to replace them

Blade assemblies, gaskets, jars, and drive couplings are all standard replaceable parts on mainstream blenders. The rubber drive coupling on the motor shaft is designed to wear instead of the motor; if the blade stops spinning under load while the motor runs, that coupling is usually the cheap fix.

Frequently asked questions

What is the fastest way to clean a blender?

Warm water plus one drop of soap, blend 30 seconds, rinse. Done before the residue dries, it beats any scrubbing session.

Why does my blender jar smell?

Residue under the lid gasket and around the blade base. Disassemble those parts and wash them; a baking-soda blend removes lingering odor.

My jar leaks from the bottom—is it broken?

Usually it is a worn or misthreaded blade gasket. Replacing the gasket or blade assembly fixes most leaks for a few dollars.

Can I blend ice in any blender?

Only in blenders rated for ice, and always with some liquid unless the manual says otherwise. Dry ice-crushing chips cheap blades.
